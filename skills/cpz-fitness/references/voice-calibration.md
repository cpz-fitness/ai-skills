## 1. Behavioral Specificity ("The Behavioral Autopsy")

**The gap this closes:** generated copy tends to name a feeling or a pattern in the abstract ("you might feel discouraged," "you're not lazy, your approach is broken"). That's true, but it's generic-true — it could describe anyone. Specificity is what makes a reader think *this person has actually watched this happen*, not *this person has a plausible theory about people like me*.

**The device:** instead of naming the pattern, narrate the sequence. Concrete units — sessions, weeks, meals, specific moments — not feelings.

**Before (abstract, still on-brand):**
> "You're not lazy. Your approach is broken."

**After (behavioral autopsy):**
> "Week one: five sessions, every rep logged. Week two: three sessions, one meal you weren't planning. Week three: one, maybe two — and you've started checking the mirror less to see progress, more to see if you've already lost it. Week four: nothing. The reason you'll give yourself is that you got busy. The actual reason is that the urgency that started it already burned out."

**When to reach for it:** hooks and diagnosis beats for Pillars 4 and 5 especially (habit cycles, comparison loops) — anywhere the content is naming a pattern the reader will recognize from their own life. Don't force it into every post; a 15-second one-liner doesn't have room for a sequence. It's a diagnosis-beat tool, not a hook-length tool.

## 2. Never Quote the Slogan

**The gap this closes:** when brand language is sitting right there in the brief ("lifting to be seen," "confidence over compliance"), the safest move for generated copy is to reach for it directly. That's exactly what makes it read like it's satisfying a brief instead of saying something.

**The rule:** the tagline and core-promise language are the internal compass, not lines to ship. Dramatize the concept through a scene or a specific claim. Never cite it back to the reader.

**Before:**
> "...because you deserve to be seen."

**After:**
> "...he was still hiding at 165 pounds heavier and hiding at 40 pounds lighter. The number never fixed it."

Add this to the root Vocabulary Guide's "Avoid these patterns" list.

## 3. The Reframe Architecture (reusable across all 5 pillars)

**The gap this closes:** this move already happens by instinct in some Pillar 4 hooks ("You're not lazy. Your approach is broken.") but it isn't written down anywhere as a general-purpose structure — which means it shows up when it shows up, not on purpose.

**The four beats:**

1. **Name the surface complaint** — exactly how the client would say it, no editorializing yet.
2. **Reject it as the real problem** — one plain line. "That's not actually what's happening."
3. **Name the actual mechanism** — specific, behavioral or psychological, not vague ("the urgency that started it burned out," not "you lost motivation").
4. **Land on a structural fix** — a design decision, a sequencing choice, a screening step. Never "try harder," never "just be consistent." The fix is something built, not something willed.

**Mapped to each pillar:**

| Pillar | Surface complaint | Real mechanism | Structural fix |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| 1. Data-Driven | "BMI says I'm unhealthy" | Wrong tool for the question | A better metric, named |
| 2. Workout Tips | "I don't have time to train" | The plan assumes a gym day that doesn't exist | A constraint-first program |
| 3. Nutrition | "I can't stick to a diet" | The diet made food a ledger | Protein anchor, not a meal plan |
| 4. Consistency | "I keep quitting in week 3" | Motivation-launched starts have a shelf life | Week 1 programmed submaximal on purpose |
| 5. Body Image | "I'll go shirtless once I'm in shape" | It was never a body problem | A program that works both tracks at once |

Use this as a structural check on any diagnosis-style post, not a template to fill in mechanically — the four beats should feel like one continuous thought, not four labeled slides.

## 4. CTA by Intention, Not by Default

**The correction driving this:** there's no house-default CTA. A typed link is real friction on a phone mid-scroll; a comment keyword is close to zero friction and it's also an engagement signal the algorithm rewards. The right CTA is a function of what the post is doing, not a fallback.

**Friction tiers (low to high):**

| Tier | Mechanic | Best for |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Lowest friction | Comment a keyword | Top-of-funnel / reach-intent posts — Interrupt-phase, polarizing or connection posts where the goal is engagement and algorithmic reach |
| Mid friction | DM a keyword | Posts where the reader is already leaning in — a diagnosis post that clearly named their pattern. Starts a real conversation, not just a data point |
| Highest friction | Typed link / link in bio | Bottom-of-funnel — the reader has already self-selected as warm (repeat engager, post-DM-conversation, high-intent content like a program breakdown) |

**Rule of thumb:** if the post's job is reach, ask for a comment. If the post's job is starting a conversation, ask for a DM keyword. Only ask for the link once someone's already most of the way to a yes.

This replaces "link in bio" as a fallback in `cpz-carousels` and `cpz-reels` — see the CTA tables in each skill's reference file, which now point back here.

## Exemplar Lines (from the July 2026 30-Day Content Plan)

Reference-quality, not to be reused verbatim in new posts — study the mechanism, not the wording.

> "Every gay man I've coached who said he'd go shirtless 'once he got his body right' was still saying it two years later — and the body wasn't the problem."
— *Behavioral specificity + reframe architecture beat 1–2, in one line.*

> "I spent three years training hard and still turned the lights off every single time — because no amount of muscle fixed what I actually believed about myself."
— *Self-implication first, with a specific, unglamorous detail ("turned the lights off") instead of a general claim about shame.*

> "The idea that you need to earn the right to be seen by building the body first is the belief that keeps gay men stuck the longest — and no generic hypertrophy plan is going to dismantle it."
— *Names and rejects worth-conditional framing directly — doubles as a Content Safety Rule–aligned example, not just a voice one.*

> "Age isn't why gay men in their 40s feel invisible — it's the story they've been telling themselves since their 30s that nobody ever gave them a structure to rewrite."
— *Reframe architecture beats 1–3 in a single sentence, structural fix implied rather than stated (earns the reveal in the caption body).*

> "Week one: five sessions, every rep logged... Week four: nothing."
— *The Behavioral Autopsy device, full sequence. Use as the template shape for diagnosis beats, not the literal weeks/numbers.*

## Proposed, Not Yet Adopted: Funnel-Phase Tagging

This is a demo of how the CSV's funnel structure *could* overlay the existing 5-pillar system — not yet folded into the content calendar workflow. Shown here so it can be evaluated before deciding whether to adopt it.

The CSV tags every post on two axes in addition to pillar:

- **Phase** — where in the funnel the post sits: **Interrupt** (pattern-break, stops the scroll, no ask yet) → **Inspire** (proof, story, credibility-building) → **Invite** (direct ask, highest-intent CTA).
- **Intention** — what the post is optimizing for: **More Reach** or **More Leads**.

These aren't a replacement for the 5 pillars — a post is still Pillar 4 (Consistency) *and* Interrupt-phase *and* Reach-intention. It's a second and third axis layered on top, and it's what lets the CTA-by-intention logic above actually get applied consistently instead of chosen post-by-post from feel.

**What it would look like layered on the existing pillar rotation** (illustrative, one week):

| Day | Pillar | Phase | Intention | CTA tier |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Mon | 4 — Consistency | Interrupt | Reach | Comment keyword |
| Tue | 1 — Data-Driven | Interrupt | Reach | Comment keyword |
| Wed | 5 — Body Image | Inspire | Reach | Comment keyword |
| Thu | 5 — Body Image | Inspire | Leads | DM keyword |
| Fri | 3 — Nutrition | Invite | Leads | Link in bio |

**What adopting it would require:** adding two columns to wherever the content calendar lives (currently the CPZ Content Ideas database in Notion), and a rule for how many Invite/Leads posts run per week relative to Interrupt/Reach ones (the CSV runs roughly 1 Invite for every 2–3 Interrupt/Inspire — worth confirming that ratio is deliberate before copying it).

**Not doing yet:** actually adding these fields to the Notion database or the weekly rotation pattern in `cpz-research/SKILL.md`. That's a separate decision once the shape above has been reviewed.
